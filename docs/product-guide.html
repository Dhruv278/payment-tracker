What it is

One place to bill clients and see where every payment stands, without chasing emails.

Projects & invoices

Create a project with its total price and currency. When a piece of work is done, send an invoice for that part: title, amount, and optionally your Wise link and the invoice PDF.

Client portal

Clients sign in, see what's due, pay with Wise, and upload the Wise receipt. They see their projects, invoices, notes and history, and nothing else.

Verification & real earnings

You check the transfer and record what actually reached you, in any currency (e.g. client paid $400, you received ₹33,200). Only you see this.

Notes, history, reminders

Meeting notes on each project (optionally emailed), a complete history of every event, and one-click payment reminders.

Money trail & history

Every invoice moves through three states, and every step is recorded.

Sent → Confirmation received → Paid

  1. You send an invoice. The client gets an email with an Upload payment confirmation button, plus Pay with Wise and the PDF if you added them. History: “Invoice sent”.
  2. Optional reminder. On an unpaid invoice, Send payment reminder re-sends the invoice email (at most once every 24 hours). History: “Payment reminder sent”.
  3. Client uploads the Wise receipt. You get an email. History: “Client uploaded a payment confirmation (paid on …)”.
  4. You verify or reject (or, if the money already arrived, use Client already paid you? on the invoice to mark it paid without waiting for an upload). Rejecting asks the client for a new confirmation with your reason. Verifying marks the invoice Paid and emails the client. History: “Payment confirmed”.
  5. You record what you actually received (any currency, after fees). History: “You received ₹… Private”. You can correct it later; the correction is logged too.

History also records: project started, status changes, price changes (with the old price), cancelled invoices (the record stays even though the invoice is removed) and notes added. Deleting a note removes its history entry.

Privacy is enforced in the database (row-level security), not only in the pages: a client signed in with their own session cannot read private rows, other clients' data or another developer's data even through the API.

Logins & accounts

Your developer account (first time on the live site)

  1. Open https://dhruv-billing.vercel.app/signup and sign up with the email in DEVELOPER_EMAILS.
  2. Click the link in the “Confirm your email” message. It works on any device.
  3. You land on the dashboard as the developer. Next time, sign in at /login.
Create your developer account before sharing the site. Anyone who signs up with an address in DEVELOPER_EMAILS becomes a developer, and email confirmation is what stops someone else from claiming it.

Clients

Invite (recommended)

Clients → Invite a client. They get a branded email, choose a password and land in their portal. No approval step.

Self sign-up

They sign up at /signup and confirm their email. You get an email and approve them on Clients. Until then they see a “waiting for approval” page.

Forgot password

/forgot-password sends a reset link that works on any device. If an invite expires, open the client and use Send a new sign-in link.

Adding another developer

Add their email to DEVELOPER_EMAILS in Vercel (comma-separated), redeploy, and have them sign up. They start with an empty workspace.

Removing a developer

Taking an email out of DEVELOPER_EMAILS does not remove access: the account stays a developer. Delete the user in Supabase → Authentication → Users (this also deletes their projects, invoices and history), or ask for their projects to be handed over first.

Database

Postgres on Supabase. The schema lives in supabase/migrations/ and is applied in order.

MigrationWhat it adds
0001_init.sqlprofiles, projects, milestones (invoice titles), payment_requests (invoices), payment_earnings (private net received), RLS policies, private documents storage bucket
0002_project_notes.sqlproject_notes: notes on a project, readable by its developer and client
0003_activity.sqlactivity: the history / money trail, with client_visible for private rows

Security model

  • Row-level security is on for every table, with read-only policies: developers read their own rows; approved clients read rows on their own projects (and only client_visible history).
  • All writes go through server actions that check role and ownership, then use the secret key. There are no insert/update/delete policies, so the API can't be used to change data directly.
  • Files (invoice PDFs, receipts) are in a private bucket, served through 10-minute signed links, max 4 MB.

Applying migrations

npx supabase link --project-ref xtnvuxnlyimnmsddhwyf
npx supabase db push        # applies any new files in supabase/migrations

Or paste each new migration file into Supabase → SQL Editor and run it. Backups: Supabase → Database → Backups (daily on paid plans). For a manual copy: npx supabase db dump -f backup.sql.

Auth email templates

supabase/templates/confirmation.html and recovery.html use token_hash links so they work on any device. They are set on the live project (Authentication → Emails → Templates).

Change live auth settings (site URL, SMTP, templates) in the Supabase dashboard. Don't run npx supabase config push from this repo: supabase/config.toml holds the local settings (localhost URLs, Mailpit) and would overwrite production.

Deploy from scratch

How the live site was set up, if you ever need to do it again (e.g. a new Supabase project).

  1. Supabase project. npx supabase login, then npx supabase projects create payment-tracker --region ap-south-1 (or create it in the dashboard).
  2. Schema. npx supabase link --project-ref <ref> and npx supabase db push.
  3. Auth settings (Authentication → URL Configuration and Emails): Site URL = your app URL; Redirect URLs = https://your-app/**; confirm email ON; custom SMTP = smtp.gmail.com, port 465, your Gmail and app password; templates from supabase/templates/.
  4. Gmail app password. Google Account → Security → 2-Step Verification → App passwords.
  5. Vercel. Import the GitHub repo (or npx vercel link), add the environment variables above, deploy. Set NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL to the final URL and redeploy.
  6. First login. Sign up with your developer email, then invite your first client.

Day-to-day & troubleshooting

SituationWhat to do
Client didn't get the invoice emailOpen the invoice → Send payment reminder (re-sends it). Check their spam folder. Check Gmail's “Sent” folder.
Client says the confirm/invite link expiredClients → client → Send a new sign-in link.
Wrong amount recorded as receivedInvoice page → Correct this record. The correction appears in history.
Invoice sent by mistakeInvoice page → Cancel invoice (until paid). The client gets a cancellation email; history keeps the record.
Need to move a project to another clientOnly possible before it has invoices or notes, so nothing written for one client is shown to another.
Can't find an email you sent to yourselfGmail often files mail sent from and to the same address under Sent or All Mail instead of the inbox. Real clients get it normally.
Rotating the Gmail app passwordUpdate it in both places: Vercel GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD (app emails, then redeploy) and Supabase → Authentication → Emails → SMTP (login emails).
No emails at allCheck GMAIL_USER / GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD in Vercel and the SMTP settings in Supabase Auth; Vercel → Logs shows [email] errors.
Site errors after a quiet weekSupabase free tier paused the project. Dashboard → Restore.

Good to know

  • Each invoice is paid in full (no partial payments). Amounts are never added across currencies.
  • New self sign-ups are visible to every developer until one approves them.
  • Notes are plain text; line breaks and simple “- ” lists are kept.
  • Times in history show in each viewer's own time zone.
